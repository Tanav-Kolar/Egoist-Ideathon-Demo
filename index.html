Coathook — a licence that travels with the work.

Set terms once in your AI Passport. Your work is fingerprinted at publish. Any copy — recompressed, renamed, re-uploaded — still resolves to your current, live terms. The fingerprinting below is real and runs in your browser.

Play all three roles in order: ① a creator sets a scoped, expiring permission in their AI Passport vault → ② a platform fingerprints their upload and binds it to that policy → ③ anyone can check a copy of the work and get its live licence status, without learning who the creator is. Try checking a recompressed copy at the verify step — it still matches, because the fingerprint comes from the pixels, not the file.

01 Set your permission

This is your standing policy — held in your AI Passport vault, not stamped into any file. Every work you publish points back to it, and you can change it at any time. There's no separate "revoke" switch: narrowing the scope or turning off AI training is revocation, because every check reads the live policy.

02 Publish & fingerprint

Connect the platforms where you publish — select as many as apply — then upload an image once. On publish, the fingerprint is computed from the pixels and the same fingerprint → policyRef pointer is registered for every connected platform simultaneously: one work, one pointer, wherever it travels. Only the pointer is stored — never your policy, never the image, never your name.

Illustrative integration model — connectors shown are not live production integrations. Select multiple to publish to all of them at once; your terms travel with the fingerprint, not any one platform.

About this demo
The perceptual hash and Hamming matching are real and run in your browser — nothing is uploaded.
Platform connectors are an illustrative integration model, not live production integrations.
The resolver here is client-side (localStorage). In production it is a hardened, optionally federated service — identity is protected via unlinkable references.
The permission is evidentiary/checkable, not DRM — it enables compliant checking and portable proof, it does not physically prevent misuse.
What's real here: the perceptual hash (DCT-based pHash over a 32×32 luma block) and Hamming-distance matching run live in your browser. A recompressed or renamed copy produces a near-identical fingerprint and matches.
What's illustrative: platform connectors are a shown integration model, not live production links. In production the resolver is a hardened service; identity is protected by unlinkable references, and the guarantee is checkable/evidentiary, not DRM.
